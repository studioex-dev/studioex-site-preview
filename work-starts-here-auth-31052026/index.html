About the work

The most important work we do is not for ourselves. It’s for the people around us. This piece is a celebration of collective humanity.

Created from hundreds of fragments of vintage fashion magazines and mounted onto a reclaimed London road works sign, the work transforms symbols of status, individuality and aspiration into a single image of community, support and shared responsibility. Every torn piece loses its original identity to become part of a bigger picture, a visual metaphor for society itself.

The composition is inspired by a photograph taken during a religious festival in the Philippines, where a young boy is carried above a densely packed crowd. Surrounded by countless hands, shoulders and bodies, he is protected not by one individual but by the collective strength of many. The scene captures a universal truth: we move further, safer and stronger when we carry one another.

The road sign itself acts as a canvas and a statement. Typically used to redirect traffic and warn pedestrians of road works, a necessary inconvenience, it is reimagined here as a reminder that progress is never achieved alone. Every achievement, every journey and every success rests upon unseen networks of support, sacrifice and cooperation.

By using fragments of Vogue magazines, publications often associated with individual image and personal identity, Blueprint creates an intentional contrast. The cuttings no longer represent singular people, brands or aspirations; together they become a portrait of collective care.

At its core, Work Starts Here is about the communities that lift us up when we cannot carry ourselves. It is about protecting the vulnerable, sharing burdens and recognising that no one succeeds in isolation. In a world increasingly defined by division and individualism, the work serves as a reminder that our greatest strength has always been each other.
